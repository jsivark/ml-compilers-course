05Calculus and gradients

Autodiff is the chain rule applied mechanically. Learn it by hand on scalars first, then for vectors and matrices, because your compiler will generate backward passes.

about 20–32 h · difficulty ●●●○○

After this module you can

  • After this module you can derive gradients of matmul, softmax and cross-entropy with correct shapes.
  • After this module you can state when forward-mode autodiff is cheaper than reverse-mode, in terms of input and output counts.
  • After this module you can check any gradient with finite differences and locate which term is wrong.
  • After this module you can explain why the backward of a broadcast is a sum and the backward of a sum is a broadcast.

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Watch

    3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus ch. 1-2: The essence of calculus; The paradox of the derivative

    Chapter 2 is the next lesson, /lessons/derivatives.

  2. Watch

    3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus ch. 3-4: Derivative formulas through geometry; Visualizing the chain rule and product rule

    Chapter 3 is /lessons/derivatives-power-rule. Pause on the chain rule section and redo it on paper for sin(x^2).

  3. Read

    Karpathy, Hacker's guide to Neural Networks, Chapter 1: Real-valued Circuits

    Code the add, multiply and sigmoid gates with forward and backward in Python, then check each against a numerical gradient.

  4. Read

    Parr & Howard, The Matrix Calculus You Need For Deep Learning: Introduction through Generalization of the Jacobian

    Covers the scalar rule review, partial derivatives, and the Jacobian. Work the Jacobian examples by hand.

  5. Read

    Parr & Howard: element-wise binary operators, scalar expansion, vector sum reduction, the chain rules

    The sum-reduction and scalar-expansion results are the backward rules for sum and expand in your autograd.

  6. Read

    Parr & Howard: the gradient of neuron activation and of the neural network loss function

    Derive dL/dW and dL/db for one linear+ReLU layer and check them numerically.

  7. Do

    Sasha Rush, Autodiff Puzzles: all puzzles

    Open the Colab notebook from the README. Each problem gives a tensor function (id, cosine, mean, cumsum, sort, matmul, 2D conv, and others); you write its derivative and the notebook tests it.

  8. Do

    Gradient check harness

    Write gradcheck(f, x) in NumPy using the centered difference, float64 and relative error as the CS231n notes describe. Use it on your matmul, softmax and log-softmax derivations.

Build

Finish all Autodiff Puzzles. Then write one NumPy file with forward and backward for matmul, sum(axis), broadcast add, ReLU, softmax and cross-entropy, each passing your gradcheck at relative error below 1e-6 in float64.

Portfolio project: Forward-mode vs reverse-mode autodiff: implement both and measure

Implement forward-mode AD with dual numbers and reverse-mode AD on a tape, over the same small expression language (scalar ops and NumPy arrays). Construct functions with n inputs and m outputs and measure time and memory as n and m vary, demonstrating the n-vs-m crossover; then compute a Hessian-vector product by composing the two (forward-over-reverse).

Deliverables

  • repo with both AD modes, a shared gradcheck, and tests against PyTorch or JAX
  • README with plots of runtime and memory vs n and m for both modes, and the HVP verified against finite differences
  • a 600–1200 word write-up explaining JVPs, VJPs, why deep learning uses reverse mode, and what the tape costs in memory

Rubric

Solid
Both modes agree with PyTorch to 1e-8 in float64 on at least 10 test functions; crossover demonstrated in a plot.
Exceptional
Implements HVP and uses it in a few steps of Newton-CG on a small problem, adds gradient checkpointing to the tape and plots the memory/compute trade-off, and relates the design to JAX's jvp/vjp.

References: Baydin et al., Automatic Differentiation in Machine Learning: a Survey

If you get stuck

  • Shape errors: write the shape next to every term. dL/dA must have A's shape, which tells you where the transposes go.
  • Gradcheck stuck around 1e-3: use float64, centered differences, a step near 1e-5, and relative error. float32 will not reach 1e-6.
  • Softmax overflow: subtract the row max before exp. The gradient does not change.
  • ReLU gradcheck fails at 0 because ReLU is not differentiable there. Use inputs that are not within the step size of 0.

Ready to move on when

  • Derive the derivative of sigmoid and write it in terms of sigmoid itself.
  • For C = A @ B, write dL/dA and dL/dB in terms of dL/dC, with shapes.
  • Show why the gradient of a broadcast is a sum over the broadcast axes, using a (3,) + (2,3) example.
  • Derive why softmax followed by cross-entropy has gradient p - y with respect to the logits.
  • All Autodiff Puzzles pass in the notebook.

Going deeper (optional)

  • MIT 18.S096 Matrix Calculus for Machine Learning and Beyond (IAP 2023) — Lectures 1-3 treat derivatives as linear operators, Jacobians, and finite-difference checks.
  • The Matrix Cookbook — Formula lookup only; no derivations.
  • Chris Olah, Calculus on Computational Graphs: Backpropagation — Forward-mode vs reverse-mode on a small graph.
