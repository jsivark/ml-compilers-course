05Calculus and gradients

Autodiff is the chain rule applied mechanically. Learn it by hand on scalars first, then for vectors and matrices, because your compiler will generate backward passes.

about 20–32 h · difficulty ●●●○○

After this module you can

  • After this module you can derive gradients of matmul, softmax and cross-entropy with correct shapes.
  • After this module you can state when forward-mode autodiff is cheaper than reverse-mode, in terms of input and output counts.
  • After this module you can check any gradient with finite differences and locate which term is wrong.
  • After this module you can explain why the backward of a broadcast is a sum and the backward of a sum is a broadcast.

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Watch

    3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus ch. 1-2: The essence of calculus; The paradox of the derivative

    Chapter 2 is the next lesson, /lessons/derivatives.

  2. Watch

    3Blue1Brown, Essence of Calculus ch. 3-4: Derivative formulas through geometry; Visualizing the chain rule and product rule

    Chapter 3 is /lessons/derivatives-power-rule. Pause on the chain rule section and redo it on paper for sin(x^2).

  3. Read

    Karpathy, Hacker's guide to Neural Networks, Chapter 1: Real-valued Circuits

    Code the add, multiply and sigmoid gates with forward and backward in Python, then check each against a numerical gradient.

  4. Read

    Parr & Howard, The Matrix Calculus You Need For Deep Learning: Introduction through Generalization of the Jacobian

    Covers the scalar rule review, partial derivatives, and the Jacobian. Work the Jacobian examples by hand.

  5. Read

    Parr & Howard: element-wise binary operators, scalar expansion, vector sum reduction, the chain rules

    The sum-reduction and scalar-expansion results are the backward rules for sum and expand in your autograd.

  6. Read

    Parr & Howard: the gradient of neuron activation and of the neural network loss function

    Derive dL/dW and dL/db for one linear+ReLU layer and check them numerically.

  7. Do

    Sasha Rush, Autodiff Puzzles: all puzzles

    Open the Colab notebook from the README. Each problem gives a tensor function (id, cosine, mean, cumsum, sort, matmul, 2D conv, and others); you write its derivative and the notebook tests it.

  8. Do

    Gradient check harness

    Write gradcheck(f, x) in NumPy using the centered difference, float64 and relative error as the CS231n notes describe. Use it on your matmul, softmax and log-softmax derivations.

Build

Finish all Autodiff Puzzles. Then write one NumPy file with forward and backward for matmul, sum(axis), broadcast add, ReLU, softmax and cross-entropy, each passing your gradcheck at relative error below 1e-6 in float64.

Portfolio project: kiln v0.2: autograd, both modes, numerically stable

kiln, the main project · v0.2 adds a Python front end over the C core, with reverse-mode autograd · builds on v0.1 · next: v0.3

Give kiln a Python front end whose Tensor wraps the v0.1 C core, and add reverse-mode autograd on a tape. Also implement forward mode with dual numbers over the same ops, build functions with n inputs and m outputs, and measure time and memory as n and m vary to show the crossover. Then make it numerically sound: implement softmax, log-sum-exp and cross-entropy in naive and stable forms, show inputs where the naive form overflows, and write a gradcheck that runs in float64 and reports how error grows in float32.

Deliverables

  • kiln v0.2 tagged in your repo: Tensor over the C core, reverse and forward mode, and a gradcheck
  • README with runtime and memory plots vs n and m for both modes, and a table of naive vs stable softmax, log-sum-exp and cross-entropy on extreme inputs
  • a 600–1200 word write-up explaining JVPs, VJPs, why deep learning uses reverse mode, and why the stable forms work

Rubric

Solid
Both modes agree with PyTorch to 1e-8 in float64 on at least 10 test functions; crossover shown in a plot; stable forms finite on inputs where the naive forms return inf or nan.
Exceptional
Implements HVP and uses it in a few steps of Newton-CG on a small problem, adds gradient checkpointing to the tape and plots the memory/compute trade-off, and relates the design to JAX's jvp/vjp.

References: Baydin et al., Automatic Differentiation in Machine Learning: a Survey

If you get stuck

  • Shape errors: write the shape next to every term. dL/dA must have A's shape, which tells you where the transposes go.
  • Gradcheck stuck around 1e-3: use float64, centered differences, a step near 1e-5, and relative error. float32 will not reach 1e-6.
  • Softmax overflow: subtract the row max before exp. The gradient does not change.
  • ReLU gradcheck fails at 0 because ReLU is not differentiable there. Use inputs that are not within the step size of 0.

Ready to move on when

  • Derive the derivative of sigmoid and write it in terms of sigmoid itself.
  • For C = A @ B, write dL/dA and dL/dB in terms of dL/dC, with shapes.
  • Show why the gradient of a broadcast is a sum over the broadcast axes, using a (3,) + (2,3) example.
  • Derive why softmax followed by cross-entropy has gradient p - y with respect to the logits.
  • All Autodiff Puzzles pass in the notebook.

Reference implementations

Make a real attempt first. When you are stuck, or after you finish, compare your work with these.

  • micrograd engine.py (Karpathy Hacker's guide gates and the scalar gradcheck)
    Reference backward rules for add, mul, pow, relu as closures; compare your local-gradient formulas line by line.
  • PyTorch derivatives.yaml (Build: matmul, sum(axis), broadcast add, softmax, cross-entropy backward)
    The production table of backward formulas for every ATen op; search for mm, sum, expand, softmax and log_softmax to check your derivations.
  • mattjj/autodidact (Project: reverse-mode AD on a tape)
    A small teaching reimplementation of HIPS autograd with both a tape and vector-Jacobian product definitions for NumPy ops.
  • JAX Autodidax (Project: forward-mode, reverse-mode and forward-over-reverse HVP)
    Builds forward mode (jvp), reverse mode via linearize and transpose, and their composition from scratch; the jvp and vjp sections match the project's Hessian-vector product.

Optional. The best talks, papers, code and writing on this module topic, from people who shaped the field.

Lectures

  • CSC321 Lecture 10: Automatic Differentiation · Roger Grosse
    Slides that walk through building a minimal autodiff library in the style of autograd, with the vjp-per-primitive structure.
  • Lecture 3: Automatic Differentiation & Backprop (CS4787) · Cornell CS4787 (Chris De Sa)

  • CS480/680 notes: Automatic Differentiation · Yaoliang Yu (University of Waterloo)
    Compact 2024 notes with a clean complexity comparison of forward and reverse mode; work through the time/space tradeoff section.

Courses

  • CMU 10-414/714 Deep Learning Systems · Tianqi Chen, Zico Kolter
    Lectures 4-5 on automatic differentiation build reverse mode as extending the computational graph, the design your compiler will use.
  • Learn autodiff (Parts 1 and 2: scalars, then vectors and reverse mode) · Simone Scardapane
    Build reverse-mode autodiff step by step, from scalar graphs to vector Jacobians; do Part 2 after the Parr and Howard notes.

Videos

  • What is Automatic Differentiation? · Ari Seff
    A clear 15-minute visual comparison of numerical, symbolic, forward and reverse mode with the n-vs-m cost argument.

Papers

  • Learning representations by back-propagating errors · Rumelhart, Hinton, Williams
    The 1986 Nature paper that popularized backprop; four pages, read it to see the generalized delta rule in its original form.

Books

  • Parallel Computing and Scientific Machine Learning (18.337 notes) · Chris Rackauckas
    The forward-mode and reverse-mode AD lectures implement dual numbers and adjoints and explain when each wins.
  • Matrix Calculus (for Machine Learning and Beyond) · Paige Bright, Alan Edelman, Steven G. Johnson
    The MIT 18.S096 notes as a 2025 arXiv book; read the derivatives-as-linear-operators chapter and the forward vs reverse (adjoint) mode chapter.

Blogs and articles

  • Matrix Calculus online tool · Laue, Mitterreiter, Giesen
    Type a matrix expression and get its derivative symbolically; use it to check your hand derivations of dL/dW.

Code to read

  • HIPS/autograd · Maclaurin, Duvenaud, Johnson
    The NumPy autodiff library that inspired JAX; read autograd/numpy/numpy_vjps.py for vjp rules of broadcasting and reductions.
  • JAX ad.py (JVP and transpose machinery) · Google JAX team
    See how a real system derives reverse mode by transposing linear JVPs; focus on JVPTrace and backward_pass.

More reading

  • MIT 18.S096 Matrix Calculus for Machine Learning and Beyond (IAP 2023) — Lectures 1-3 treat derivatives as linear operators, Jacobians, and finite-difference checks.
  • The Matrix Cookbook — Formula lookup only; no derivations.
  • Chris Olah, Calculus on Computational Graphs: Backpropagation — Forward-mode vs reverse-mode on a small graph.
