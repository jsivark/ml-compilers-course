26Tensor cores, fast GEMM, flash attention

Fast GEMM and attention are where tiling, register blocking, async copies and tensor-core instructions all meet. Your compiler must emit them, not you.

about 50–70 h · difficulty ●●●●●

After this module you can

  • Generate register-tiled GEMM kernels with float4 loads and cp.async double buffering from your scheduler.
  • Emit mma.sync with fragment layouts that pass a single 16x8x16 tile test against NumPy.
  • Generate a FlashAttention-2 style forward kernel with online softmax that matches a float64 reference.

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    siboehm CUDA matmul worklog: Kernels 1-3

    Naive, global memory coalescing, shared-memory cache-blocking; generate each from your IR.

  2. Implement

    Shared-memory tiled GEMM from your scheduler

    LOCAL buffers plus barrier; compare your GFLOPS with the post's kernel 3.

  3. Read

    siboehm Kernels 4-6 (1D and 2D blocktiling, vectorized SMEM/GMEM access)

    Register tiling and float4 loads; then skim kernels 9 (autotuning) and 10 (warptiling). The post skips 7 and 8.

  4. Implement

    Register tiling + vectorized loads

    UPCAST on GPU = per-thread register tiles; emit float4/half2 loads.

  5. Read

    PTX ISA: warp-level matrix multiply-accumulate (mma.sync) fragments

    The fragment layout tables for mma.m16n8k16 with fp16 inputs.

  6. Read

    spatters, Implementing a fast Tensor Core matmul on the Ada Architecture

    Kernel 1.0 (naive mma) through 3.0 (N-stage cp.async pipeline), with ldmatrix and a permuted shared-memory layout in 2.0. Reaches 89.5% of cuBLAS on an RTX 4090 for one problem size.

  7. Read

    tinygrad TensorCore definitions

    The TensorCore dataclass: dtype_in, dtype_out and per-thread fragment layouts (frag_a, frag_b, frag_c), which the scheduler uses to target mma.

  8. Implement

    mma.sync emission + double buffering

    Emit inline-PTX mma.sync and cp.async double buffering from your scheduler; fp16 4096^3.

  9. Measure

    GEMM vs cuBLAS

    Benchmark cuBLAS via PyTorch outside your compiler; target >= 70%.

  10. Read

    FlashAttention §3.1 Algorithm 1, then FA2 §3.1-3.2

    Online softmax tiling (FA1 Alg. 1), then FA2's loop reordering and fewer non-matmul FLOPs (https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08691).

  11. Read

    flash-attention-minimal: flash.cu

    115-line forward kernel; map each loop to a range in your IR.

  12. Implement

    Fused attention kernel

    Generate a FA2-style forward from your IR; check vs NumPy, benchmark vs PyTorch SDPA (target >= 60%).

  13. Test

    10 own tests

    Odd seq lengths, causal masking, GEMM sizes not multiple of tile.

Build

Register tiling, vectorized and double-buffered loads, mma.sync, and a fused attention kernel emitted by your compiler.

Portfolio project: Tensor-core GEMM and flash attention: within reach of cuBLAS and FA2

Benchmark your compiler-emitted fp16 GEMM against cuBLAS on square sizes 1024-8192, and your fused attention forward against FlashAttention-2 (head dim 64, 16 heads, batch 4, seqlen 512-8192). Explain each remaining gap with ncu data.

Deliverables

  • repo with generated kernels, a shape-sweep benchmark and correctness tests vs float64 references
  • README with TFLOP/s vs size plots for GEMM and attention, with cuBLAS and FA2 lines
  • an ncu comparison of your best GEMM vs cuBLAS at 4096^3 (occupancy, pipe utilization, stall reasons)
  • a 600-1200 word write-up on the three optimizations that mattered most, with ablation numbers

Rubric

Solid
GEMM at 60% of cuBLAS at 4096^3 fp16; attention correct and faster than unfused PyTorch attention at every tested seqlen.
Exceptional
GEMM at 85% of cuBLAS and attention at 70% of FA2; causal masking supported; stall-reason analysis explains the remaining gap.

References: FlashAttention-2: Faster Attention with Better Parallelism and Work Partitioning, Simon Boehm, How to Optimize a CUDA Matmul Kernel, Nsight Compute Profiling Guide

If you get stuck

  • mma.sync results scrambled: fragment layout per thread is specific; copy the PTX ISA layout tables and test a single 16x8x16 tile first.
  • Shared-memory bank conflicts kill performance: check ncu bank-conflict counters and swizzle or pad the tile.
  • Double buffering slower: you added a sync that serializes loads and compute; use cp.async with commit/wait groups.
  • Flash attention numerics drift: online softmax must rescale the running sum and output when the max changes; test against a float64 reference.
  • Register spills (check -Xptxas -v): tile too large for the register file; lower it.

Ready to move on when

  • fp16 4096^3 GEMM >= 70% of cuBLAS
  • Flash-attention forward >= 60% of PyTorch SDPA
  • mma.sync appears in compiler-generated code, not hand-written CUDA
  • All earlier tests still pass
