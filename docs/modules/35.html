35Scheduling theory: polyhedral model and auto-schedulers

Your BEAM search picked schedules by trial. Research compilers reason about the space formally (polyhedra, dependences) or learn cost models. Grad labs and XLA/TPU teams expect you to know both camps.

about 40–60 h · difficulty ●●●●●

After this module you can

  • After this module you can model loop nests as polyhedra and check transform legality
  • After this module you can build and evaluate a learned cost model for schedule search
  • After this module you can explain the design space of auto-schedulers and their failure modes
  • After this module you can read a scheduling paper and reproduce its key experiment

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    Halide (PLDI 2013)

    Sections 3-5: algorithm/schedule separation, the tradeoff space of locality, parallelism and recompute.

  2. Read

    Learning to Optimize Halide with Tree Search and Random Programs (2019)

    Beam search + learned cost model; compare with your BEAM.

  3. Read

    Ansor (OSDI 2020)

    Sections 4-6: sketch generation, random annotation, evolutionary fine-tuning.

  4. Read

    isl tutorial (Verdoolaege)

    Sets, maps, dependences, schedule trees. Work the examples with islpy.

  5. Practice

    Pluto: polyhedral source-to-source

    Read the Pluto paper linked there; run it on a stencil and matmul and inspect tiling hyperplanes.

  6. Read

    Tiramisu (CGO 2019)

    A polyhedral compiler with a scheduling language: the bridge between Halide and isl.

  7. Implement

    Dependence analysis in your compiler

    Using islpy, compute dependences for your loop IR and prove which tile/permute transforms are legal.

  8. Implement

    Learned cost model for your BEAM search

    Collect (schedule features, runtime) pairs, train a small GBDT/MLP, and use it to prune search.

Build

islpy-based legality checking for your loop transforms, plus a learned cost model that cuts BEAM search time.

Portfolio project: Reproduce a cost-model result on your own compiler

Reproduce the core claim of a learned-scheduling paper (Halide 2019, Ansor, or TenSet) inside your compiler: same feature ideas, your search space. Report sample efficiency curves and transfer across GPUs or across ops.

Deliverables

  • repo with the dataset of measured schedules (released), training code and integration
  • README with search-time vs. achieved-performance curves against random and plain BEAM
  • a 600–1200 word write-up on what transferred, what didn't and why

Rubric

Solid
Dataset of >=20k measured schedules, cost model with reported rank correlation, clear sample-efficiency plot
Exceptional
Shows cross-hardware transfer or a novel feature that measurably helps; written like a workshop paper

References: TenSet (NeurIPS 2021 datasets), Ansor

If you get stuck

  • isl notation feels alien: write every example by hand first, then check with islpy
  • Cost model overfits: split by program, not by schedule, or you measure leakage
  • Measurements noisy: lock clocks, warm up, take medians of repeated runs
  • Pluto build issues: use a container; the concepts matter more than the tool

Ready to move on when

  • Your compiler rejects an illegal interchange on a loop-carried dependence and accepts the legal one
  • Cost-model-guided search finds schedules within 10% of exhaustive BEAM using <=25% of the measurements
  • You can write the iteration domain and schedule of a tiled matmul as isl sets/maps
  • One-page comparison of Halide autoscheduler vs. Ansor vs. polyhedral
