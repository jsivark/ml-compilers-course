Part II · Bridge

16Performance engineering

An ML compiler is only as good as the code it emits. MIT 6.172 covers work, caches, vectorization and measurement; Aalto PPC has you optimize one kernel step by step on CPU, which is what kernel codegen must do automatically.

about 40–60 h · difficulty ●●●●○

After this module you can

  • Compute your CPU's theoretical peak FLOP/s and a kernel's arithmetic intensity, and place it on a roofline.
  • Optimize a CPU SGEMM with blocking, packing, SIMD and threads, and report GFLOP/s per stage.
  • Use perf (or Instruments on macOS) counters to explain a speedup, such as cache misses dropping after a loop interchange.
  • Benchmark with warmup, repeated runs, and median plus spread.

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Watch

    MIT 6.172 Lecture 1: Introduction and Matrix Multiplication

    The speedup ladder for matmul: Python, Java, C, loop order, compiler flags, parallel loops, tiling, vectorization.

  2. Watch

    MIT 6.172 Lecture 2: Bentley Rules for Optimizing Work

    A catalog of work-reducing transforms; many of them are compiler passes.

  3. Watch

    MIT 6.172 Lecture 4: Assembly Language and Computer Architecture

    x86-64 assembly, pipelines and vector units.

  4. Watch

    MIT 6.172 Lecture 5: C to Assembly

    How clang lowers C to LLVM IR and then to assembly.

  5. Watch

    MIT 6.172 Lecture 9: What Compilers Can and Cannot Do

    What the compiler can prove and what it cannot, including aliasing, inlining and vectorization limits.

  6. Do

    MIT 6.172 HW2: Profiling Serial Merge Sort and HW3: Vectorization

    HW2 uses perf and cachegrind to profile a merge sort. HW3 has you read clang vectorization reports and get loops to vectorize.

  7. Read

    Aalto PPC Chapter 2: Case study, V0-V7 with the Asm pages

    V0 Baseline, V1 Linear reading, V2 Instruction-level parallelism, V3 Vector instructions, V4 Reuse data in registers, V5 More register reuse, V6 Prefetching, V7 Better use of cache memory.

  8. Do

    Aalto PPC exercises CP1, CP2a, CP2b, CP2c, CP3a

    CP1 CPU baseline, CP2a instruction-level parallelism, CP2b multicore parallelism, CP2c vectorization, CP3a fast solution with doubles. Submit to the grader and record runtime per version.

  9. Do

    Aalto PPC exercise IS4 (image segmentation, fast solution) or MF1 (median filter, CPU baseline)

    One more problem with a different access pattern. Optional.

  10. Read

    Algorithmica HPC: Matrix Multiplication case study

    Baseline, transposition, vectorization, register reuse, kernel design, blocking. The author reaches about 50x over naive and BLAS-level speed on a Zen 2 CPU.

  11. Build

    Hand-optimize a CPU matmul to >50% of peak

    In C with intrinsics: naive -> transposed -> blocked -> micro-kernel -> OpenMP; plot GFLOP/s per step.

Build

A benchmarked CPU SGEMM in C (callable from your Module 15 library via ctypes) with a writeup plotting GFLOP/s across 6 optimization stages against theoretical peak, plus perf counter evidence (cache misses, IPC) for each stage.

Portfolio project: CPU SGEMM reaching a stated fraction of peak, with a roofline report

Write a single-precision GEMM in C from naive loops to a packed, register-blocked, SIMD (AVX2/AVX-512 or NEON), multithreaded kernel. Document each optimization step with measured GFLOP/s and hardware counters, place each version on a roofline for your machine, and compare to OpenBLAS/Accelerate.

Deliverables

  • repo with each optimization step as a separate tagged version and a reproducible benchmark harness
  • README with GFLOP/s vs matrix size plots, a roofline chart, and cache-miss counters per version
  • a 600–1200 word write-up explaining micro-kernel design, packing, and why each step helped

Rubric

Solid
Single-thread kernel reaches 50%+ of theoretical peak on large sizes; all versions verified against a reference.
Exceptional
80%+ of peak and within 10–20% of OpenBLAS/Accelerate, with a tuned micro-kernel justified by register/latency arithmetic and multithreaded scaling analysis.

References: how-to-optimize-gemm, Brendan Gregg: Flame Graphs

If you get stuck

  • Noisy timings: warm up, repeat, report median and spread; pin threads to cores where possible.
  • If the compiler removed your benchmark loop, consume the result or use a DoNotOptimize-style barrier.
  • Check vectorization with -Rpass=loop-vectorize (clang) or -fopt-info-vec (gcc) instead of guessing.
  • Choose block sizes from your own L1/L2 sizes and measurements.

Ready to move on when

  • You can compute your CPU's theoretical peak FLOP/s from cores, frequency, SIMD width and FMA units
  • Your final matmul is within 2x of OpenBLAS/numpy at 2048x2048
  • You can explain from perf counters why loop order i-k-j beats i-j-k
  • Aalto CP3a passes the grader at the benchmark time limit

Reference implementations

Make a real attempt first. When you are stuck, or after you finish, compare your work with these.

  • matmul.c (Aman Salykov) (Hand-optimized CPU matmul build and project)
    A pure-C BLIS-style SGEMM (packing, cache blocking, AVX2 FMA micro-kernel, OpenMP) that the author benchmarks against OpenBLAS; the write-up is at salykova.github.io/matmul-cpu.
  • Fast Multidimensional Matrix Multiplication on CPU from Scratch (Simon Boehm) (Build: GFLOP/s per stage with perf evidence)
    Goes from naive to tiled, multithreaded C++ matmul with perf counter evidence for each step, close to the per-stage report you write.
  • how-to-optimize-gemm (FLAME group) (Micro-kernel and packing steps)
    Robert van de Geijn's group's step-by-step GEMM optimization with each version as its own file, from naive to packed 4x4 SIMD micro-kernel.
  • BLIS (Project, closing the gap to OpenBLAS)
    The production BLIS framework; read the haswell sgemm micro-kernel to see what the last 2x looks like.

Going deeper

Optional. The best talks, papers, code and writing on this module topic, from people who shaped the field.

Courses

  • Performance-Aware Programming · Casey Muratori
    A long course on reading assembly and measuring throughput from first principles; the SIMD and caching parts fit this module.

Talks

  • CppCon 2015: Chandler Carruth "Tuning C++: Benchmarks, and CPUs, and Compilers! Oh My!" · Chandler Carruth
    Live perf and benchmark work, including the DoNotOptimize/clobber trick from the stuck list.
  • "Performance Matters" by Emery Berger · Emery Berger
    Why layout effects make naive benchmarks lie, and causal profiling with Coz; watch before trusting small speedups.
  • CppCon 2014: Mike Acton "Data-Oriented Design and C++" · Mike Acton
    The case for designing around data layout and cache lines; the cache-miss arithmetic in the first half is the useful part.

Papers

  • Anatomy of High-Performance Matrix Multiplication · Kazushige Goto, Robert van de Geijn
    The paper that defines the packing and blocking layers every fast CPU GEMM uses; Sections 4-6 give the block-size reasoning.
  • Roofline: An Insightful Visual Performance Model · Samuel Williams, Andrew Waterman, David Patterson
    The original roofline paper; use it to draw the roofline your project report requires.

Books

  • Performance Analysis and Tuning on Modern CPUs · Denis Bakhvalov
    Free book on perf counters, Top-Down microarchitecture analysis and measurement noise; read the Top-Down chapter.
  • What Every Programmer Should Know About Memory · Ulrich Drepper
    Deep coverage of caches, TLBs and prefetching; Section 6 has a worked matmul cache-blocking example.
  • Agner Fog's optimization manuals · Agner Fog
    Instruction tables and microarchitecture guides; use the tables to compute FMA throughput for your peak FLOP/s.

Blogs and articles

  • Linux perf examples · Brendan Gregg
    Copy-paste perf recipes for counters, sampling and flame graphs.
  • Performance Matters (Travis Downs) · Travis Downs
    Microarchitecture investigations such as store buffer and AVX frequency effects; read the posts on AVX-512 downclocking.

Code to read

  • uops.info · Andreas Abel, Jan Reineke
    Measured latency, throughput and port usage per instruction per CPU; check your micro-kernel's FMA and load ports.
  • Compiler Explorer · Matt Godbolt
    Inspect the assembly your loops produce under different flags to confirm vectorization.
  • google/benchmark · Google
    A microbenchmark harness with warmup, repetition statistics and DoNotOptimize built in.

More reading

  • MIT 6.172 Lecture 8: Analysis of Multithreaded Algorithms — Work/span analysis.
  • How to Optimize a CUDA Matmul Kernel (Simon Boehm) — Bridge to GPU version in Module 17.

Stretch project

Write a BLIS-style SGEMM with a hand-scheduled AVX-512 or NEON micro-kernel and an autotuner that searches block sizes (MC, KC, NC, MR, NR) per machine. Reach at least 85% of single-core theoretical peak and match OpenBLAS within 5% on square and skinny shapes (2048x2048, 4096x64), with a Top-Down perf breakdown explaining the remaining gap.
