37Accelerator architecture: systolic arrays, TPUs, dataflows

A compiler for a TPU-like accelerator needs a model of the hardware: dataflow, buffer sizes and array utilization. This module builds that model and a backend for it.

about 40–60 h · difficulty ●●●●○

After this module you can

  • Estimate accelerator cycles and energy from dataflow, array size and buffer sizes
  • Write a compiler backend that tiles matmul and conv for a non-GPU target

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    In-Datacenter Performance Analysis of a Tensor Processing Unit (ISCA 2017)

    The systolic matrix unit, the roofline comparison with CPU and GPU, and why response-time limits shaped the design.

  2. Read

    TPU v4: An Optically Reconfigurable Supercomputer for Machine Learning with Hardware Support for Embeddings (ISCA 2023)

    Optical circuit switches and SparseCores; list what changed since TPUv1.

  3. Read

    Efficient Processing of DNNs: A Tutorial and Survey (Sze et al.)

    Section V.B, energy-efficient dataflows: weight-stationary, output-stationary, no-local-reuse and row-stationary.

  4. Read

    Eyeriss: An Energy-Efficient Reconfigurable Accelerator for Deep CNNs (JSSC 2017)

    Row-stationary dataflow and energy cost per memory level.

  5. Read

    MIT 6.5930/1 Hardware Architecture for Deep Learning (Sze, Emer)

    Public course page with syllabus and reading list; lectures and labs are on MIT Canvas. Use the reading list to choose papers on dataflow and mapping.

  6. Practice

    Timeloop/Accelergy

    Follow the Tutorials & Examples on the site; map a conv and a matmul onto a modeled accelerator and compare mappings by energy and cycles.

  7. Read

    Gemmini (Berkeley)

    Read the README Architecture section: an open systolic-array generator with an ISA and software stack.

  8. Implement

    Cycle-level systolic array simulator

    Python or C++: NxN PEs, weight- and output-stationary modes, SRAM buffers, DMA; count cycles and accesses.

  9. Implement

    Compiler backend for your simulator

    Lower your compiler's matmul/conv to tiled instructions for the simulator; pick tile sizes by buffer capacity.

Build

A cycle-level systolic array simulator with two dataflows, plus a backend in your compiler that targets it.

Portfolio project: Design-space exploration of a transformer accelerator

Extend your simulator with a vector unit and a two-level on-chip memory, then sweep array size, buffer size and dataflow for one GPT-2 small transformer layer in prefill (seq 512) and decode (batch 1-16). Use your compiler to generate the mappings and plot cycles, energy and an area proxy (PE count plus SRAM bytes).

Deliverables

  • repo with simulator, compiler backend and sweep scripts
  • README with Pareto plots and a validation section against analytic models
  • a 600–1200 word write-up recommending a design for decode-heavy serving

Rubric

Solid
Validated simulator, at least 50 design points, a clear prefill vs. decode comparison
Exceptional
Results cross-checked against Timeloop on at least 3 design points, plus a quantified finding such as switching dataflow between prefill and decode

References: Timeloop/Accelergy, TPU v4

If you get stuck

  • Simulator too slow: validate cycle-exact on small cases, then compute per-tile cycles analytically
  • Cycle counts off: count the (N-1) fill and drain cycles of the systolic wavefront
  • Mapping space too large: prune by buffer capacity first, as Timeloop's mapper does
  • Energy numbers arbitrary: use relative per-level access costs from the Sze et al. survey

Ready to move on when

  • Simulator matches numpy outputs, and its matmul cycle count is within 5% of your analytic formula including fill and drain
  • A plot of utilization vs. matrix shape, with the drop for small and skinny matrices explained
  • Your compiler's tiling beats naive tiling in cycles on a ResNet-50 3x3 conv layer
  • You can explain from the TPU papers why TPUs use large systolic arrays and bf16
