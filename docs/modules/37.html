37Accelerator architecture: systolic arrays, TPUs, dataflows

A compiler is only as good as its model of the hardware. TPU, Trainium and startup accelerator teams hire people who can reason about dataflow, buffer sizing and utilization, not only GPUs.

about 40–60 h · difficulty ●●●●○

After this module you can

  • After this module you can model accelerator performance and energy from first principles
  • After this module you can compare dataflows and choose mappings
  • After this module you can write a compiler backend for a non-GPU target
  • After this module you can discuss TPU generations in depth

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    In-Datacenter Performance Analysis of a TPU (TPUv1, ISCA 2017)

    Systolic matrix unit, the roofline section, why latency mattered more than throughput.

  2. Read

    TPU v4 (ISCA 2023)

    Optical circuit switches, SparseCores, the scale-up story; contrast with v1.

  3. Read

    Efficient Processing of DNNs: A Tutorial and Survey (Sze et al.)

    Sections on dataflows: weight-, output-, row-stationary.

  4. Read

    Eyeriss (JSSC 2017)

    Row-stationary dataflow and energy accounting per memory level.

  5. Watch

    MIT 6.5930 Hardware Architecture for Deep Learning

    Follow the lectures on dataflow and mapping; do the Timeloop labs if available.

  6. Practice

    Timeloop/Accelergy

    Map a conv and a matmul onto a modeled accelerator; compare mappings by energy and cycles.

  7. Read

    Gemmini (Berkeley)

    Read the README architecture section: a real open systolic generator with a software stack.

  8. Implement

    Cycle-level systolic array simulator

    Python or C++: NxN PEs, weight- and output-stationary modes, SRAM buffers, DMA; count cycles and accesses.

  9. Implement

    Compiler backend for your simulator

    Lower your compiler's matmul/conv to tiled instructions for the simulator; pick tile sizes by buffer capacity.

Build

A cycle-level systolic array simulator with two dataflows, plus a backend in your compiler that targets it.

Portfolio project: Design-space exploration of a transformer accelerator

Extend your simulator with a vector unit and on-chip memory hierarchy, then explore array size, buffer size and dataflow for a transformer layer (prefill vs. decode). Use your compiler to generate the mapping and produce Pareto curves of perf/area-proxy/energy.

Deliverables

  • repo with simulator, compiler backend and sweep scripts
  • README with Pareto plots and a validation section against analytic models
  • a 600–1200 word write-up recommending a design for decode-heavy serving

Rubric

Solid
Validated simulator, >=50 design points, clear prefill-vs-decode insight
Exceptional
Results cross-checked against Timeloop or published TPU numbers, plus a non-obvious finding (e.g. a dataflow switch per phase) with quantified benefit

References: Timeloop, TPU v4

If you get stuck

  • Simulator too slow: simulate per tile analytically after validating cycle-exact on small cases
  • Off-by-skew cycle counts: remember the (N-1) fill and drain of a systolic wavefront
  • Mapping space huge: use Timeloop's mapper ideas, constrain by buffer capacity first
  • Energy numbers meaningless: use relative per-level costs from Eyeriss/Sze tables

Ready to move on when

  • Simulator matches numpy results and an analytic cycle count for matmul within 5%
  • You can show utilization vs. matrix shape and explain the drop for small or skinny matrices
  • Your compiler's tiling for the simulator beats naive tiling on a ResNet layer
  • You can explain why TPUs use bf16 and large systolic arrays
