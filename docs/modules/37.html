Part IV · Frontier

37Numerics and low precision

LLM decoding is limited by memory bandwidth, so serving stacks store weights and activations in fp8, int4 or MX formats. This module covers how these formats lose precision and how GPTQ, AWQ and SmoothQuant recover it.

about 40–60 h · difficulty ●●●●○

StepsBuildProjectHelpGo deeper

After this module you can

  • Derive and emulate fp8 and MX formats bit-exactly
  • Implement GPTQ and AWQ and locate which layers cause accuracy loss
  • Write a fused dequantize-matmul kernel and measure it against fp16

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    Fabien Sanglard, Floating point visually explained

    Warm-up. Then derive by hand the max value, min normal and epsilon of bf16, fp16, fp8 E4M3 and E5M2.

  2. Read

    FP8 Formats for Deep Learning (Micikevicius et al.)

    Section 3: E4M3 vs E5M2 encodings, special values (E4M3 has no infinity) and exponent bias. Section 4: training and inference results and per-tensor scaling.

  3. Read

    OCP Microscaling (MX) Formats v1.0 spec

    Section 5: MX block structure, element types (MXFP8, MXFP6, MXFP4, MXINT8) and the E8M0 shared scale. Section 6: dot products and conversion from fp32 vectors.

  4. Read

    Microscaling Data Formats for Deep Learning

    Training and inference results with MX formats.

  5. Read

    LLM.int8(): 8-bit Matrix Multiplication for Transformers at Scale

    Outlier feature dimensions and the mixed-precision decomposition that keeps them in fp16.

  6. Read

    SmoothQuant: Accurate and Efficient Post-Training Quantization for LLMs

    Moving activation outliers into weights with per-channel scales; derive the migration strength alpha.

  7. Read

    GPTQ: Accurate Post-Training Quantization for Generative Pre-trained Transformers

    Section 3 (layer-wise quantization, OBQ) and Section 4 (the GPTQ algorithm: arbitrary order, lazy batch updates, Cholesky reformulation).

  8. Read

    AWQ: Activation-aware Weight Quantization for LLM Compression and Acceleration

    Per-channel scaling chosen from activation magnitudes; compare its assumptions with GPTQ.

  9. Implement

    FP8 training recipe in Transformer Engine

    Read the FP8 section of the user guide and the delayed-scaling recipe (amax history, scale update); run the FP8 example on a small model.

  10. Implement

    Bit-exact format emulators

    numpy emulators for bf16, fp8 E4M3/E5M2 and MXFP4 with round-to-nearest-even and saturation, tested against torch.float8_e4m3fn and torch.float8_e5m2.

  11. Implement

    Quantized matmul kernel

    W4A16 Triton kernel that loads packed int4 weights and dequantizes in registers; add it to your compiler as a dtype.

Build

Format emulators, GPTQ and AWQ written from scratch and applied to a 1-2B model (for example Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen2.5-1.5B), and a W4A16 kernel used by your compiler.

Portfolio project: Precision study: how low can a real LLM go?

Skill project

Take Llama-3.2-1B or Qwen2.5-1.5B and evaluate bf16, fp8 (per-tensor vs. per-channel), MXFP8, MXFP4 (emulated) and int4 (GPTQ vs. AWQ). Measure WikiText-2 perplexity, accuracy on one downstream task (for example HellaSwag via lm-evaluation-harness) and tokens/s with your kernels. Find the most sensitive layers and propose a mixed-precision recipe.

Deliverables

  • repo with emulators, quantizers, kernels and an eval harness
  • README with accuracy-vs-bytes Pareto plot and per-layer sensitivity heatmap
  • a 600–1200 word write-up of findings and the recommended recipe

Rubric

Solid
At least 6 configurations evaluated on both metrics with measured speed; scripts reproduce every number
Exceptional
A mixed-precision recipe that beats uniform int4 or MXFP4 on both perplexity and bytes per parameter, with kernel speedups measured

References: OCP MX spec v1.0, GPTQ

Help

If you get stuck

  • Emulator off by one ulp: you truncated instead of rounding to nearest even, or mishandled subnormals
  • GPTQ diverges: add dampening to the Hessian diagonal; the paper uses 1% of the average diagonal value
  • Perplexity differs from papers: match context length, dataset split and tokenizer
  • Quantized kernel slow: dequantize in registers after loading packed ints, not into shared memory

Ready to move on when

  • Emulators bit-match torch.float8_e4m3fn and torch.float8_e5m2 casts on 1e6 random fp32 values, including subnormals and out-of-range values
  • Your 4-bit GPTQ model (group size 128) is within 0.5 WikiText-2 perplexity of a reference GPTQ implementation at the same context length
  • Your W4A16 kernel beats the fp16 matmul at batch size 1 on the model's MLP shapes
  • You can explain why E4M3 has no infinity and what that means for scale selection
Reference implementations (6)

Make a real attempt first. When you are stuck, or after you finish, compare your work with these.

  • IST-DASLab/gptq (Build: GPTQ from scratch)
    Authors' code; gptq.py implements lazy batch updates and the Cholesky inverse in about 150 lines.
  • mit-han-lab/llm-awq (Build: AWQ from scratch)
    Authors' AWQ search for per-channel scales plus W4A16 CUDA kernels.
  • mit-han-lab/smoothquant (SmoothQuant alpha derivation)
    Authors' scale-migration code and activation-statistics collection.
  • microsoft/microxcaling (Bit-exact format emulators (MXFP4))
    Reference PyTorch emulation of MX formats from the MX paper authors, with rounding and E8M0 scale handling.
  • IST-DASLab/marlin (Quantized matmul kernel)
    A near-ideal FP16xINT4 GPU kernel; compare its weight layout and dequant tricks with your Triton W4A16 kernel.
  • torchao (Build and project: fp8/MX/int4 evaluation)
    PyTorch's quantization library with int4 weight-only, fp8 and MX code paths to diff against.

Talks, papers, code and writing (16)

Courses

  • MIT 6.5940 TinyML and Efficient Deep Learning (EfficientML.ai) · Song Han
    Optional. Lectures and labs on quantization, pruning and efficient LLM inference.

Papers

  • What Every Computer Scientist Should Know About Floating-Point Arithmetic · David Goldberg
    Rounding error, guard digits and the IEEE rules your emulators must follow; focus on the rounding and relative error sections.
  • QuaRot: Outlier-Free 4-Bit Inference in Rotated LLMs · Ashkboos et al.
    Hadamard rotations remove outliers so activations quantize to 4 bits; a natural next step after SmoothQuant.
  • SqueezeLLM: Dense-and-Sparse Quantization · Kim et al.
    Non-uniform quantization guided by sensitivity; read for the layer sensitivity analysis the project asks for.
  • Extreme Compression of Large Language Models via Additive Quantization (AQLM) · Egiazarian et al.
    Vector quantization reaching 2 bits per weight; contrast with scalar int4.
  • The Era of 1-bit LLMs: All Large Language Models are in 1.58 Bits · Ma et al.
    Ternary training as the limit of low precision; read to see what changes when quantization moves into training.
  • DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report · DeepSeek-AI
    Section 3.3 describes fine-grained fp8 training with tile-wise scaling at production scale.
  • Recipes for Pre-training LLMs with MXFP8 · Mishra et al. (NVIDIA)
    2025 study of MXFP8 pre-training; focus on why E4M3 everywhere works and the scale-factor rounding change versus the OCP default.
  • Characterization and Mitigation of Training Instabilities in Microscaling Formats · Su, Kwun, Gil, Kakade, Anand
    Nearly 1,000 small runs showing where MX training diverges; read the gradient-bias analysis around layer-norm parameters.
  • Pretraining Large Language Models with NVFP4 · NVIDIA
    How 4-bit training is made to match FP8: 16-element blocks, E4M3 scales, stochastic rounding and Hadamard transforms.

Blogs and articles

  • Comparing Floating Point Numbers, 2012 Edition · Bruce Dawson
    ULP-based comparison you need when testing emulators against torch dtypes.
  • A Visual Guide to Quantization · Maarten Grootendorst
    Illustrated walk-through of symmetric/asymmetric quantization, GPTQ and GGUF; good orientation before the papers.
  • Floating-Point 8: An Introduction to Efficient, Lower-Precision AI Training · NVIDIA
    E4M3 vs E5M2, per-tensor vs block scaling, and MXFP8 on Blackwell; read before the numerics project.

Code to read

  • llama.cpp ggml-quants.c · ggml-org
    The k-quant block formats used by most local LLM inference; read the Q4_K packing and dot-product routines.
  • bitsandbytes · bitsandbytes-foundation
    The LLM.int8() and NF4 implementations; read the outlier decomposition path.
