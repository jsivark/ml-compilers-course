34Industrial ML compilers: MLIR, XLA/StableHLO, TorchInductor, TVM

Every big lab's stack is one of these. You built a toy compiler; now read real designs, see which choices they made differently, and get fluent in MLIR, the lingua franca of accelerator compilers.

about 45–70 h · difficulty ●●●●○

After this module you can

  • After this module you can define an MLIR dialect, write passes and lower to LLVM
  • After this module you can read XLA HLO and Inductor output and explain their fusion decisions
  • After this module you can compare compiler designs in an interview with specifics
  • After this module you can navigate a large compiler codebase to find where a decision is made

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    MLIR: Scaling Compiler Infrastructure (Lattner et al.)

    Sections 2-4: dialects, regions, progressive lowering.

  2. Implement

    j2kun mlir-tutorial: Getting started

    Build with Bazel or CMake; get mlir-opt running.

  3. Implement

    j2kun: Running and testing a lowering

    lit/FileCheck tests; you'll use them for everything.

  4. Implement

    j2kun: Writing our first pass

    An affine loop-unroll pass and a rewrite pattern.

  5. Implement

    j2kun: Defining a new dialect

    TableGen ops/types: the basis for your toy tensor dialect.

  6. Implement

    j2kun: Canonicalizers and declarative rewrite patterns

    DRR patterns for algebraic simplification.

  7. Implement

    j2kun: Dialect conversion

    Type converters and conversion targets: lower your dialect to arith/linalg.

  8. Implement

    j2kun: Lowering through LLVM

    Finish the pipeline to an executable.

  9. Read

    OpenXLA architecture and StableHLO

    HLO, fusion and layout assignment; then skim the StableHLO spec at openxla.org/stablehlo.

  10. Read

    TorchInductor design post (Jason Ansel)

    Define-by-run loop IR, scheduling, Triton codegen. Then the PyTorch 2 paper sections on Dynamo and Inductor.

  11. Read

    PyTorch 2 paper (ASPLOS 2024)

    Sections 3-5: graph capture via bytecode, guards, Inductor.

  12. Read

    TVM (OSDI 2018)

    Sections 3-5: tensor expressions, schedules, learned cost model.

Build

A toy 'tg' MLIR dialect (add, mul, matmul, reduce) with canonicalization and a lowering to linalg -> loops -> LLVM, tested with FileCheck.

Portfolio project: Port your compiler's frontend onto MLIR (or upstream-style Inductor study)

Make your course compiler emit your MLIR dialect, then reuse upstream linalg tiling/fusion and the GPU dialect to reach a GPU, and compare against your hand-written backend. Alternative: a deep empirical study of TorchInductor fusion decisions on 5 HuggingFace models with a minimal patch that improves one case.

Deliverables

  • repo with the dialect, passes, FileCheck tests and an end-to-end example
  • README with performance comparison vs. your original backend and vs. torch.compile
  • a 600–1200 word write-up on what MLIR made easier or harder than your own IR

Rubric

Solid
An MLP runs end to end through MLIR with correct results; 20+ lit tests
Exceptional
A small transformer block runs on GPU via MLIR within 2x of torch.compile, or the Inductor patch is opened upstream with benchmarks

References: j2kun mlir-tutorial, MLIR Toy tutorial

If you get stuck

  • Build takes forever: use the tutorial's pinned LLVM commit and ccache; build once, release mode
  • TableGen errors are opaque: start from the tutorial's dialect and change one thing at a time
  • Dialect conversion 'failed to legalize': mark the right ops legal/illegal and check your type converter covers block args
  • Inductor output overwhelming: start with a two-op function and TORCH_LOGS=output_code,schedule

Ready to move on when

  • mlir-opt runs your pass pipeline and the resulting binary computes a correct matmul
  • At least 10 FileCheck tests cover canonicalization and lowering
  • One-page comparison table: your compiler vs. XLA vs. Inductor vs. TVM on IR levels, fusion, scheduling, autotuning
  • You can dump and read Inductor's generated Triton for a small model (TORCH_LOGS=output_code)
