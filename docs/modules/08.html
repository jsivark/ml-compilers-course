08Deep learning core

MLPs, CNNs, initialization, normalization and optimizers. These are the models your compiler must train correctly and fast.

about 50–70 h · difficulty ●●●●○

After this module you can

  • After this module you can implement a tensor-level autograd with broadcasting-aware gradients and train real models on it.
  • After this module you can diagnose training from activation and gradient statistics and fix it with initialization, normalization or optimizer choice.
  • After this module you can write backward passes for batchnorm, cross-entropy and convolution by hand.
  • After this module you can implement SGD-momentum and Adam and explain their update rules.

Build

Extend your Module 7 engine (or your Module 6 NDArray) into a tensor-level autograd with matmul, broadcast ops, sum, log-softmax and cross-entropy, plus SGD-momentum and Adam. Train a 2-layer MLP on MNIST to >97% test accuracy, and compare loss curves with an identical PyTorch model.

Portfolio project: MiniTorch-scale framework: train a CNN and profile where time goes

Grow your Module 8 autograd into a small framework with Conv2d (via im2col), MaxPool, BatchNorm, Dropout, a DataLoader and a module system, and train a small CNN on CIFAR-10 to at least 60% test accuracy. Profile one training step and produce a breakdown by op, then speed up the top op (for example a vectorized im2col or a C extension) and report the gain.

Deliverables

  • repo with the framework, gradient tests against PyTorch for every op, and the training script
  • README with loss and accuracy curves vs an identical PyTorch model, plus a per-op time breakdown before and after optimization
  • a 600–1200 word write-up on what dominated training time and why, linking it to the arithmetic intensity of each op

Rubric

Solid
Every op gradient-checked against PyTorch; CIFAR-10 at least 60% test accuracy; profile breakdown and one optimization with measured speedup.
Exceptional
At least 70% on CIFAR-10, the training curve tracks PyTorch's within noise given the same seed and init, and the write-up uses FLOP and byte counts to predict which ops are compute-bound vs memory-bound.

References: Horace He, Making Deep Learning Go Brrrr From First Principles

If you get stuck

  • Broadcast-add gradient has the wrong shape: sum the upstream gradient over the broadcast dimensions (keepdims, then reshape) to match the input.
  • makemore Part 4 batchnorm backward: derive it on paper with shapes first; the bessel correction (n-1) in the variance trips people up.
  • MNIST stuck at around 10% accuracy: check data normalization, label alignment, and that the softmax is over the right axis.
  • Adam not matching PyTorch: check bias correction and that eps is added after the sqrt.
  • CS231n assignments take far longer than estimated; budget the time and do not skip the conv backward.

Ready to move on when

  • makemore Part 4 notebook: every manual gradient matches PyTorch (cmp prints exact/approx True).
  • Explain why Kaiming init uses gain/sqrt(fan_in) and what happens to tanh activations without it.
  • Explain BatchNorm train vs eval behaviour and its backward pass in one paragraph.
  • Compute output shape and parameter count of a conv layer given input, kernel, stride, padding.
  • CS231n A1 two-layer net reaches the target validation accuracy.
