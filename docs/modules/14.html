Part II · Bridge

14Term rewriting, e-graphs and lambda calculus

tinygrad's optimizer is a pattern-matching rewrite engine over a graph IR (UOps and PatternMatcher); e-graphs are the principled version of that idea. Lambda calculus gives you the formal vocabulary for substitution, binding and evaluation order that rewriting depends on.

about 25–40 h (lambda interpreter 4-6 h, egg talk and paper 4-6 h, e-graph 8-12 h, rewriter and comparison 8-12 h) · difficulty ●●●●○

StepsBuildProjectHelpGo deeper

After this module you can

  • Implement capture-avoiding substitution and normal-order reduction.
  • Build an e-graph with hashconsing, union-find, rebuilding and equality saturation, and extract terms with a cost function.
  • Explain where tinygrad's PatternMatcher and graph_rewrite get stuck that equality saturation does not.

Steps

Work through these in order. Each step points at one specific unit.

  1. Read

    Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: The Lambda Calculus, sections 1, 2 and 4

    Section 2 covers syntax and free/bound variables; section 4 covers reduction and reduction strategies. Skip sections 5-9.

  2. Do

    Write a lambda calculus interpreter with capture-avoiding substitution

    In Python, implement terms, capture-avoiding substitution and normal-order reduction; encode Church numerals and check that 2+3 reduces to 5.

  3. Watch

    egg: Fast and Extensible Equality Saturation (POPL 2021 talk, 30 min)

    Linked from the egg homepage. Watch for e-classes, rebuilding, equality saturation and extraction.

  4. Read

    egg paper: Fast and Extensible Equality Saturation

    Read the introduction, background and rebuilding sections; skim the case studies. The intro's (a*2)/2 example shows why destructive rewriting depends on rule order.

  5. Do

    egg tutorials (Rust), or a Python reimplementation

    The docs have three parts: background, getting started, explanations. Either follow them in Rust or write a tiny Python e-graph (union-find, hashcons, rebuild) for arithmetic.

  6. Read

    tinygrad docs: UOp, the IR tinygrad rewrites

    Read how tinygrad represents computation as UOp(op, src, arg) graphs. The pattern matcher is not documented there; read it in ops.py in the next step.

  7. Do

    Write a pattern-rewrite engine for an expression DAG

    Read the UPat and PatternMatcher classes and graph_rewrite in this pinned ops.py, then write your own: patterns with wildcards, a fixed-point rewrite, and constant-folding and algebraic rules.

  8. Build

    Compare greedy rewriting vs equality saturation

    Run the same rule set through your greedy rewriter and your e-graph on 10 expressions; record each case where greedy ends at a costlier result.

Build

A Python rewrite toolkit with (1) a PatternMatcher-style greedy rewriter over a hash-consed expression DAG and (2) a mini e-graph with equality saturation and cost-based extraction, both applied to arithmetic/tensor-index expressions, with a report showing cases where saturation finds better results than greedy rewriting.

Portfolio project: kiln v0.6: an equality-saturation optimizer over the kiln graph

kiln, the main project · v0.6 adds a rewrite engine (e-graph) over the kiln graph IR · builds on v0.5.1 · next: v0.7

Build an e-graph optimizer that runs on the kiln graph IR, starting with its tensor algebra (matmul, add, transpose, elementwise ops) with 15+ rules such as transpose-of-transpose, (AB)^T = B^T A^T, matmul associativity and distributivity. Extract with a FLOP cost model and check it finds the optimal parenthesization for 10 matrix chains of length 4-6 (compare to the textbook O(n^3) dynamic program).

Deliverables

  • repo with e-graph core (union-find, hashcons, rebuild), 15+ rules, and cost-based extraction
  • README with plots of e-graph size vs iterations and FLOP reductions on 10 test expressions, with numerical-equivalence checks in NumPy
  • a 600–1200 word write-up explaining equality saturation, rule explosion control, and extraction tradeoffs

Rubric

Solid
Matches the dynamic-programming optimal FLOP count on all 10 chains, NumPy results agree to 1e-6 relative error, and saturation stops cleanly at the node limit.
Exceptional
ILP or tree-DP extraction compared with greedy extraction, the same rules run in egg for comparison, and random testing of every rule for soundness.

References: egg: Fast and extensible equality saturation, egg source

Help

If you get stuck

  • Rewrites looping on a+b->b+a need a termination ordering or an e-graph instead of destructive rewriting.
  • E-graph blowup: set node and iteration limits; associativity plus commutativity together is the usual cause.
  • Wrong results after unions mean congruence was not restored; follow the egg paper's rebuilding section.
  • Substitution bugs are almost always variable capture: use de Bruijn indices or fresh names.

Ready to move on when

  • Substituting y into (\x.\y.x) renames the bound y, giving \y'.y
  • Church numeral 2+3 and 2*3 reduce to the normal forms of 5 and 6
  • Your e-graph proves (a*2)/2 == a with rules a*2->a<<1, (a*b)/c->a*(b/c), x/x->1, x*1->x, even though the shift rule also fires
  • Your greedy rewriter terminates on a rule set that contains a+b->b+a
Reference implementations (4)

Make a real attempt first. When you are stuck, or after you finish, compare your work with these.

  • egg test suite for math rewrites (e-graph step and check (a*2)/2 == a)
    egg's own arithmetic rule set with constant folding, conditional rewrites and analyses; compare your rules and extraction results to it.
  • tinygrad symbolic rewrite rules (pattern-rewrite engine step)
    The real PatternMatcher rule sets for constant folding and algebraic simplification at the commit the module pins; check your greedy rules against these.
  • egglog-python: egglog from Python (build and project: tensor-expression e-graph)
    Python bindings for egglog with documented examples; run your project rule set through it to cross-check saturation results.
  • Tensat source code (project: tensor algebra optimizer)
    Equality saturation for tensor graph rewriting with ILP extraction, written on egg; the research-grade version of the module project.

Talks, papers, code and writing (19)

Lectures

  • Equality saturation lecture slides (McGill COMP 520, 2025) · McGill University
    Lecture slides covering e-nodes, e-classes and rebuilding, with DialEgg (egglog for MLIR) as a case study.

Courses

  • egglog tutorial · egraphs-good
    Hands-on chapters that write an arithmetic optimizer and then a linear algebra one in egglog; do the first two chapters as exercises.

Talks

  • aegraphs: Acyclic E-graphs for Efficient Optimization in a Production Compiler (slides) · Chris Fallin
    Invited-talk slides on the e-graph variant Cranelift ships; focus on why it avoids full saturation and how extraction is kept cheap.

Papers

  • Equality Saturation: a New Approach to Optimization · Ross Tate, Michael Stepp, Zachary Tatlock, Sorin Lerner
    The paper that introduced equality saturation for compiler optimization; read the motivating examples on phase ordering.
  • Equality Saturation for Tensor Graph Superoptimization (Tensat) · Yichen Yang et al.
    Applies e-graphs to TASO-style tensor rewrites and shows extraction by ILP; read the multi-pattern rules and extraction sections.
  • Better Together: Unifying Datalog and Equality Saturation (egglog) · Yihong Zhang et al.
    Combines e-graphs with Datalog for analyses; read sections 2-3 for the language design.
  • Slotted E-Graphs: First-Class Support for (Bound) Variables in E-Graphs · Schneider, Rossel, Shaikhha, Goens, Koehler, Steuwer (PLDI 2025)
    Connects the lambda calculus and e-graph halves of this module: how to do equality saturation over terms with binders without de Bruijn indices.
  • Hydra: Generalizing Peephole Optimizations with Program Synthesis · Mukherjee and Regehr
    Generalizes concrete rewrites into verified rules; read section 2 for how rules are generalized and checked.

Books

  • Types and Programming Languages · Benjamin Pierce
    Chapters 5-7 formalize untyped lambda calculus and de Bruijn indices with an implementation.
  • The Implementation of Functional Programming Languages · Simon Peyton Jones
    Free book on graph reduction; chapters 2 and 11-12 connect lambda calculus to sharing in graph rewriting.

Blogs and articles

  • Cranelift aegraph design proposal (RFC) · Chris Fallin (Bytecode Alliance)
    How a production compiler adopted an acyclic e-graph for its mid-end and bounded blowup; read the motivation and design sections.
  • Egraphs Part I · Philip Zucker
    A from-scratch e-graph in a few dozen lines; his blog has many follow-ups on e-matching and egglog.
  • Lambda Calculus · Ben Lynn
    An interactive interpreter page covering normal-order reduction and Church encodings; compare its results with your interpreter.
  • What's in an e-graph? · Max Bernstein
    Builds a small e-graph with union-find and congruence step by step in code; a gentler on-ramp before the egg paper.
  • Custom Data Structures in E-Graphs · UW PLSE
    A 2026 post on egglog container support (sets, maps, vectors) inside e-graphs; read it once you hit the limits of pure term rewriting.
  • Automatic verification of peephole optimizations (Alive) · CS 6120 course blog
    How Alive checks LLVM peephole rewrites with an SMT solver; the same idea as z3-checking your own rewrite rules.

Code to read

    A floating-point accuracy optimizer built on e-graphs; try its web demo to see rewriting with an accuracy cost function.
