Portfolio

Every module ends with a project you can publish. Taken together, they are evidence of depth: a working compiler, measured kernels, reproduced papers, merged open-source contributions, and clear technical writing. Below each project is the bar for exceptional work.

How to present it

  • One public GitHub repo per project, or one monorepo with clear folders. Each needs a README with how to run it, results tables and plots.
  • A write-up for each project on your blog. Explain the why, the measurements and what failed. This is the strongest signal you can send.
  • Pin your four best projects: usually the compiler, a GPU kernel worklog, a paper reproduction, and a merged PR.

Start here · Orientation

  1. 00Public course repo + first blog post

    Reproducible environment (uv.lock or a devcontainer) that CI builds from scratch, and a `make bench` target that writes timings to CSV for use in later modules.

Part I · Foundations

  1. 01An arena-allocated tensor runtime core in C

    Randomized property tests that compare tcore results against NumPy, benchmark results reported as median and interquartile range, and CI that runs the sanitizer build on every push.

  2. 02Compiler Explorer study: what -O2 does to tensor loops

    Uses `perf stat` or llvm-mca to explain throughput from instruction counts and port pressure, demonstrates a measurable speedup from adding `restrict` or reassociation, and predicts the speedup before measuring it.

  3. 03Single-core CPU SGEMM: from naive to a large fraction of peak

    Best version reaches at least 60% of peak or within 2x of single-threaded OpenBLAS, includes a roofline plot, and the write-up attributes each speedup with perf miss counts or FLOPs per byte.

  4. 04Blocked matrix algorithms and the cost of numerical stability

    Adds Cholesky or QR, shows growth-factor results on Wilkinson's matrix, and reports a measured speedup from tuning block size.

  5. 05Forward-mode vs reverse-mode autodiff: implement both and measure

    Implements HVP and uses it in a few steps of Newton-CG on a small problem, adds gradient checkpointing to the tape and plots the memory/compute trade-off, and relates the design to JAX's jvp/vjp.

  6. 06A view-tracking shape tracker (a mini ShapeTracker)

    Simplifies emitted index expressions (constant folding, removing mod/div when provably unneeded), supports masks for pad, and compares its design to tinygrad's ShapeTracker with references to specific code.

  7. 07A tiny graph IR for your autograd: visualize, optimize, codegen

    Generated C version gives a speedup of 100x or more over the interpreter, adds constant folding, and the write-up connects the design to torch.fx or jax.make_jaxpr.

  8. 08MiniTorch-scale framework: train a CNN and profile where time goes

    At least 70% on CIFAR-10, the training curve tracks PyTorch's within noise given the same seed and init, and the write-up uses FLOP and byte counts to predict which ops are compute-bound vs memory-bound.

  9. 09A C inference engine for GPT-2 with KV cache, benchmarked

    Decode reaches at least 50% of the memory-bandwidth bound, int8 perplexity on a held-out text stays within 1–2% of fp32, and the code is clean enough to compare favourably with llama2.c's run.c.

Part II · Bridge

  1. 10Bytecode VM for your Lox subset with a measured speedup

    C VM with computed-goto dispatch, superinstructions justified by an opcode-pair frequency profile, 20x+ speedup, and a fuzzer bug found and documented.

  2. 11Three-address IR and peephole pass for mycc, measured against naive codegen

    Also passes with --bitwise --compound, and comes within 1.2x of gcc -O0 instruction count on the chapter 9 valid programs.

  3. 12An optimizing middle-end with a measured pass pipeline

    An exhaustive search over orders of the 4 passes with the best order per benchmark reported, and a random-program fuzzer that found and fixed at least one miscompile.

  4. 13Equality-saturation optimizer for tensor expressions

    ILP or tree-DP extraction compared with greedy extraction, the same rules run in egg for comparison, and random testing of every rule for soundness.

  5. 14Tensor autograd engine that trains a real model, with gradient-checkpointing

    Checkpointing cuts peak memory by a measured factor with sqrt(n) segment analysis, HVP via forward-over-reverse verified, and results matched against PyTorch to 1e-6.

  6. 15Lazy-evaluating mini-framework with fusion and a benchmark against PyTorch CPU

    Within 2x of PyTorch CPU tokens/sec, a liveness-based memory planner with measured savings, and a visualization of the scheduled graph.

  7. 16CPU SGEMM reaching a stated fraction of peak, with a roofline report

    80%+ of peak and within 10–20% of OpenBLAS/Accelerate, with a tuned micro-kernel justified by register/latency arithmetic and multithreaded scaling analysis.

  8. 17GPU kernel suite: matmul and fused softmax vs cuBLAS

    SGEMM 80%+ of cuBLAS with warp-tiling/double buffering, softmax near bandwidth peak, profiler-backed analysis, and kernels training a model in your framework.

Part III · The Course

  1. 18z3-checked symbolic simplifier with a rewrite tracer

    Uses interval bounds as z3 assumptions so conditional rules (e.g. x%n -> x when 0<=x<n) are proven too; documents at least one unsound rule you wrote first, with its counterexample.

  2. 19Differential fuzzer: C backend vs interpreter

    Generator is driven by Hypothesis strategies so shrinking is automatic; you compare its shrunk cases against your own reducer; generated programs hit every op and every REJECTED rule at least 100 times.

  3. 20Roofline benchmark harness for your compiler

    CI flags any kernel that slows down by more than 5% between commits; adds an L1/L2 bandwidth ceiling and explains which gemm sizes fall out of cache.

  4. 21Fusion plan search with a cost model

    Cost model includes a cache-size term fitted from your Module 20 bandwidth numbers and the correlation improves measurably; one counterintuitive case explained with measured numbers.

  5. 22Property-tested view algebra with a z3-checked index simplifier

    Proves a general merge condition for reshape chains with z3, not just test cases; the simplification gives a measured speedup on conv2d in your Module 20 harness.

  6. 23MNIST, stories15M and ResNet-18 on your compiler vs PyTorch

    Within 2x of torch.compile on at least one workload, with a profile explaining the gap; transcendentals within 2 ULP; a GPT-2 124M forward pass matching Hugging Face logits to 1e-4.

  7. 24Autotuner for your CPU backend

    GEMM reaches 80% of OpenBLAS; a learned cost model (e.g. gradient-boosted trees on schedule features) reaches the random-search best in 3x fewer trials.

  8. 25GPU kernel lab: bandwidth and reduction kernels near hardware peak

    90% of bandwidth on elementwise and reduce; softmax matches or beats torch.softmax at 1024x32768.

  9. 26Tensor-core GEMM and flash attention: within reach of cuBLAS and FA2

    GEMM at 85% of cuBLAS and attention at 70% of FA2; causal masking supported; stall-reason analysis explains the remaining gap.

  10. 27Sharding planner with a communication cost model

    Cost model within 15% of measured step time; overlapping communication with compute gives a measured speedup.

  11. 28Small inference server for llama2.c models on your compiler

    Batch-1 decode reaches 50% of the bandwidth bound; paged KV cache shows a measured capacity gain; speculative speedup within 15% of the analytical prediction.

  12. 29Capstone phase 1: proposal, baseline and public harness

    Harness runs in CI; the proposal states the roofline ceiling for the target and the expected gap before any optimization code exists.

  13. 30Capstone phase 2: lab notebook and ablations

    Clear win over a strong public baseline, or a negative result with a root cause; at least one issue or PR filed upstream.

  14. 31Capstone phase 3: public technical report, talk and demo

    Someone else reproduces your headline number from the README and says so publicly, or the report is submitted to a workshop.

Part IV · Frontier

  1. 32Fused kernel library: beat torch.compile on three real ops

    At least one kernel beats torch.compile by >=1.2x end to end, e.g. a chunked fused cross-entropy that cuts peak memory by >2x, with Nsight Compute evidence for why

  2. 33bf16 MoE grouped GEMM on Hopper with a public benchmark

    Beats the cuBLAS loop on small token counts and explains why with occupancy and pipeline analysis.

  3. 34Lower your course compiler's IR through MLIR

    The MLP or a single transformer block runs on GPU via the MLIR GPU dialect within 2x of torch.compile, or the Inductor patch is opened upstream with benchmarks

  4. 35Reproduce a cost-model result on your own compiler

    Shows cross-hardware transfer or a novel feature that measurably helps; written like a workshop paper

  5. 36Precision study: how low can a real LLM go?

    A mixed-precision recipe that beats uniform int4 or MXFP4 on both perplexity and bytes per parameter, with kernel speedups measured

  6. 37Design-space exploration of a transformer accelerator

    Results cross-checked against Timeloop on at least 3 design points, plus a quantified finding such as switching dataflow between prefill and decode

  7. 38Scale a model and publish an MFU report

    >=45% MFU, or a working long-context or MoE extension, with a cost model that predicts measured step time within 15%

  8. 39Capstone: a research-grade contribution

    Accepted at a workshop (for example an MLSys or NeurIPS workshop), or a merged feature that maintainers build on, plus a talk at GPU MODE or a reading group
