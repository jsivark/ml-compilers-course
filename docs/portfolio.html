Portfolio

Every module ends with a project you can publish. Taken together, they are evidence of depth: a working compiler, measured kernels, reproduced papers, merged open-source contributions, and clear technical writing. Below each project is the bar for exceptional work.

How to present it

  • One public GitHub repo per project, or one monorepo with clear folders. Each needs a README with how to run it, results tables and plots.
  • A write-up for each project on your blog. Explain the why, the measurements and what failed. This is the strongest signal you can send.
  • Pin your four best projects: usually the compiler, a GPU kernel worklog, a paper reproduction, and a merged PR.

Start here · Orientation

  1. 00Public course repo + first blog post

    Reproducible environment (lockfile or devcontainer), a `make bench` placeholder that writes results to CSV for later modules, and a blog post that someone else in GPU MODE or online found useful enough to comment on or share.

Part I · Foundations

  1. 01An arena-allocated tensor runtime core in C

    Fuzzes the API with libFuzzer or randomized property tests against a Python reference, reports variance (median and spread) on benchmarks, and the code is clean enough that a reviewer would merge it: consistent error handling, no globals, CI running sanitizers.

  2. 02Compiler Explorer study: what -O2 does to tensor loops

    Uses `perf stat` or llvm-mca to explain throughput from instruction counts and port pressure, demonstrates a measurable speedup from adding `restrict` or reassociation, and predicts the speedup before measuring it.

  3. 03Single-core CPU SGEMM: from naive to a large fraction of peak

    Best version reaches at least 60–70% of peak or within 2x of OpenBLAS single-threaded, includes a roofline plot, and the write-up attributes each speedup quantitatively (miss counts from perf, or FLOPs per byte).

  4. 04Blocked matrix algorithms and the cost of numerical stability

    Adds a Cholesky or QR variant, shows growth-factor experiments (e.g. Wilkinson's matrix), and explains how LAPACK's getrf uses the same blocking, with a measured speedup from block size tuning.

  5. 05Forward-mode vs reverse-mode autodiff: implement both and measure

    Implements HVP and uses it in a few steps of Newton-CG on a small problem, adds gradient checkpointing to the tape and plots the memory/compute trade-off, and relates the design to JAX's jvp/vjp.

  6. 06A view-tracking shape tracker (a mini ShapeTracker)

    Simplifies emitted index expressions (constant folding, removing mod/div when provably unneeded), supports masks for pad, and compares its design to tinygrad's ShapeTracker with specific code references.

  7. 07A tiny graph IR for your autograd: visualize, optimize, codegen

    Generated C version gives a speedup of 100x or more over the interpreter, adds constant folding, and the write-up connects the design to torch.fx or jax.make_jaxpr.

  8. 08MiniTorch-scale framework: train a CNN and profile where time goes

    At least 70% on CIFAR-10, the training curve tracks PyTorch's within noise given the same seed and init, and the write-up uses FLOP and byte counts to predict which ops are compute-bound vs memory-bound.

  9. 09A C inference engine for GPT-2 with KV cache, benchmarked

    Decode reaches at least 50% of the memory-bandwidth bound, int8 perplexity on a held-out text stays within 1–2% of fp32, and the code is clean enough to compare favourably with llama2.c's run.c.

Part II · Bridge

  1. 10Bytecode VM for your Lox subset with a measured speedup

    C VM with computed-goto dispatch, superinstructions justified by an opcode-pair frequency profile, 20x+ speedup, and a fuzzer bug found and documented.

  2. 11Register allocator shoot-out in your own compiler

    Within 1.5x of gcc -O1 runtime on most benchmarks, SSA-based allocation or a provably-correct allocation checker, and a clear analysis of compile-time vs code-quality tradeoff.

  3. 12An optimizing middle-end with a measured pass pipeline

    Pass pipeline competitive with LLVM -O1 on comparable inputs, a fuzzer that found real bugs, and a principled pass-ordering experiment (e.g. search over orders) with insights.

  4. 13Equality-saturation optimizer for tensor expressions

    ILP or DP-based extraction compared with greedy, a comparison against egg on the same rules, and a rewrite rule correctness checker (random testing or SMT).

  5. 14Tensor autograd engine that trains a real model, with gradient-checkpointing

    Checkpointing cuts peak memory by a measured factor with sqrt(n) segment analysis, HVP via forward-over-reverse verified, and results matched against PyTorch to 1e-6.

  6. 15Lazy-evaluating mini-framework with fusion and a benchmark against PyTorch CPU

    Within 2x of PyTorch CPU tokens/sec, a liveness-based memory planner with measured savings, and a visualization of the scheduled graph.

  7. 16CPU SGEMM reaching a stated fraction of peak, with a roofline report

    80%+ of peak and within 10–20% of OpenBLAS/Accelerate, with a tuned micro-kernel justified by register/latency arithmetic and multithreaded scaling analysis.

  8. 17GPU kernel suite: matmul and fused softmax vs cuBLAS

    SGEMM 80%+ of cuBLAS with warp-tiling/double buffering, softmax near bandwidth peak, profiler-backed analysis, and kernels training a model in your framework.

Part III · The Course

  1. 18Proved-correct symbolic simplifier with a rewrite visualizer

    Finds and documents at least one real unsound rule (yours or in a published compiler) with a counterexample; uses bounds as z3 assumptions so conditional rules are proven too; compares against an egg-style e-graph on the same set.

  2. 19Differential fuzzer: compiler vs interpreter vs NumPy

    Generator is grammar- or Hypothesis-driven with a coverage feedback loop; reduced cases average under 10 nodes; you find a bug in a real tool (e.g. a clang fast-math or NumPy dtype corner) and report it upstream.

  3. 20Roofline-annotated benchmark dashboard

    Intensity computed automatically from the IR (not hand-entered); dashboard tracks regressions per commit and flags >5% slowdowns in CI; includes cache-level rooflines (L1/L2/DRAM).

  4. 21Fusion explorer with a cost model and ablation study

    Matches or beats tinygrad's scheduler on the same graphs on your machine; cost model refined with measured cache effects; finds a counterintuitive case (fusion slows things down) and explains it with counters.

  5. 22Property-tested view algebra with a z3-checked index simplifier

    Proves view-merge conditions in general (not just test cases); reduces index ops enough to give a measured kernel speedup; compares your expressions with tinygrad's ShapeTracker output.

  6. 23Train-and-serve benchmark: your compiler vs PyTorch on three models

    Within 2x of torch.compile on at least one model with a profile explaining the remaining gap; transcendentals within 2 ULP; adds a small model nobody gave you (e.g. a GPT-2 124M forward) and verifies it.

  7. 24Autotuner for your CPU backend

    GEMM reaches 80%+ of OpenBLAS; learned cost model cuts trials by 3x+ vs random search; tuned configs transfer across shapes with a measured penalty.

  8. 25GPU kernel lab: bandwidth and reduction kernels near hardware peak

    Reaches 90%+ of bandwidth; fused softmax beats PyTorch at some sizes; the write-up is good enough that you would publish it as a blog post.

  9. 26Tensor-core GEMM and flash attention: within reach of cuBLAS and FA2

    GEMM at 85%+ of cuBLAS and attention at 70%+ of FA2 on your GPU; causal masking and variable seqlen supported; a stall-reason analysis explains the remaining gap.

  10. 27Sharding planner with a communication cost model

    Cost model within 15% of measured; comm/compute overlap gives a measured speedup; plan search scales to a transformer block with mixed DP+TP.

  11. 28Mini inference server on your compiler

    Matches or beats gpt-fast at batch 1 on your GPU; paged KV cache shows a measured capacity gain; speculative decoding speedup matches the analytical prediction from acceptance rate.

  12. 29Capstone phase 1: proposal, baseline and public harness

    Proposal reads like a workshop paper intro; harness runs in CI; you identify the theoretical ceiling and the expected gap before starting.

  13. 30Capstone phase 2: lab notebook and ablations

    Clear win over a strong public baseline, or a well-argued negative result with root cause; at least one insight a maintainer of the baseline would want to hear about (issue or PR filed).

  14. 31Capstone phase 3: public technical report, talk and demo

    Report is good enough for a workshop submission (e.g. an MLSys/ASPLOS workshop) or gets substantive engagement from engineers at a compiler/kernel team; a stranger reproduces your results and confirms it.

Part IV · Frontier

  1. 32Fused kernel library: beat torch.compile on three real ops

    At least one kernel beats torch.compile by >=1.2x end to end, e.g. a chunked fused cross-entropy that cuts peak memory by >2x, with Nsight Compute evidence for why

  2. 33FP8 or bf16 attention/GEMM on Hopper with a public benchmark

    Matches or beats the baseline on some shapes (e.g. small-batch decode) and explains why with occupancy/pipeline analysis; upstreamable quality

  3. 34Port your compiler's frontend onto MLIR (or upstream-style Inductor study)

    A small transformer block runs on GPU via MLIR within 2x of torch.compile, or the Inductor patch is opened upstream with benchmarks

  4. 35Reproduce a cost-model result on your own compiler

    Shows cross-hardware transfer or a novel feature that measurably helps; written like a workshop paper

  5. 36Precision study: how low can a real LLM go?

    A mixed-precision recipe that Pareto-dominates uniform int4 or MXFP4, with kernel speedups measured, written up as a blog post others cite

  6. 37Design-space exploration of a transformer accelerator

    Results cross-checked against Timeloop or published TPU numbers, plus a non-obvious finding (e.g. a dataflow switch per phase) with quantified benefit

  7. 38Scale a model and publish an MFU report

    >=45% MFU, or a working long-context/MoE extension, with a scaling analysis that predicts your measured numbers

  8. 39Capstone: a research-grade contribution

    A novel result accepted at a workshop (e.g. MLSys, NeurIPS workshop) or a feature merged upstream that maintainers use, plus a talk at GPU MODE or a reading group
